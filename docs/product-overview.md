# Lumo Studio: From trusted source material to finished work

Lumo Studio is the primary Lumo workspace for turning documents, data, and questions into useful, reviewable work.

It brings source files, knowledge, AI assistance, editable artifacts, dashboards, and team review into one place. Instead of moving between a chat tool, a file drive, a document editor, and separate approval or collaboration threads, you can do the work in a durable workspace where the context and the resulting artifacts stay together.

Lumo is the assistant and product family; Lumo Studio is the main application. Knowledge, skills, workflows, and integrations extend the workspace rather than becoming separate destinations users must stitch together.

## The problem Lumo Studio solves

Important work is often spread across disconnected tools:

- Source material lives in folders, email attachments, cloud drives, and old document versions.
- Questions and instructions are separated from the files they refer to.
- AI-generated drafts can be difficult to verify, edit, or continue after the original chat ends.
- Reviewers have to discuss changes in one place and apply them somewhere else.
- Teams need to control which knowledge, integrations, and actions an assistant can use.

This makes research and document work slower than it needs to be. People spend time finding context, repeating instructions, reconciling versions, and translating feedback into edits.

Lumo Studio is designed to make that workflow continuous: bring the context into a workspace, ask for help, inspect and edit the result, involve people when judgment is needed, and preserve the work for the next step.

## How it works

A typical Lumo Studio workflow looks like this:

1. **Create or open a workspace.** A workspace is the home for the files, conversations, knowledge references, generated artifacts, and collaboration connected to a piece of work.
2. **Add context.** Upload files or folders, import selected files from Google Drive, create text files, or reference material already in the workspace.
3. **Ask Lumo to help.** Use the assistant to research, summarize, compare, draft, transform, analyze, or update work. You can point it to specific files and published knowledge bases instead of relying on an unstructured chat history.
4. **Review the work as it is produced.** Responses stream into the conversation while tool activity, generated files, dashboards, and other artifacts appear in the workspace.
5. **Stay in control.** When a task reaches a configured clarification, approval, or other human checkpoint, Lumo Studio lets you answer, approve, edit, or reject before continuing.
6. **Refine and share.** Edit supported documents, restore earlier versions, annotate content, discuss changes, collaborate with teammates, and publish or share the appropriate workspace state.

The result is not just an answer in a chat window. It is a continuing body of work that can be inspected, revised, and reused.

## Core features

### 1. Workspaces that keep context together

Each workspace provides a durable boundary for a project or area of work. It can contain:

- Files and folders
- Conversations and assistant history
- Generated documents and other artifacts
- Dashboards and reports
- Knowledge references
- Schedules for supported refresh workflows
- Shared content, comments, tasks, and review activity

Workspaces can be renamed, shared, published, copied privately, restored, or moved to the trash. Supported workflows may also synchronize or copy content between workspace states. This gives a project a clear home instead of making every task start from scratch.

### 2. AI assistance grounded in your material

Lumo is the workspace assistant. You can ask it to work with the material you provide and the knowledge you explicitly make available to the conversation. Opening a conversation does not invoke Lumo by itself; a user explicitly starts an invocation through the available Lumo action, mention, or selected reference.

Depending on the task, permissions, and enabled capabilities, Lumo can help you:

- Summarize or explain documents
- Find and compare information across files
- Draft emails, briefs, reports, and other documents
- Transform existing content into a new format
- Inspect documents and create or update workspace artifacts
- Analyze structured data and produce reports or dashboards
- Use approved skills and configured integrations
- Search the internet when that option is enabled

You can mention workspace files and knowledge in the chat, attach local files, or import selected Google Drive files. The application checks that the referenced material belongs to the workspace and that you are allowed to access it before passing it to the agent. Internet search and external integrations are available only when enabled and authorized.

### 3. A place for the output, not only the prompt

Lumo Studio is built around durable outputs. Depending on what you ask for, the result may be a document, report, data file, dashboard package, presentation-related artifact, or another workspace file.

The workspace canvas and file tree support common working formats including Markdown, HTML, images, PDFs, CSV, JSON, Plotly, Mermaid, and generated dashboard packages. Office documents can be previewed, and supported DOCX workflows provide native editing with version and revision checks.

You can download files, inspect prior versions, restore a version, and continue working from the same workspace later.

### 4. Human-in-the-loop control

Lumo Studio does not treat every AI action as something that should happen without review. Some tasks pause at a human checkpoint, for example when the assistant has prepared a plan, needs a clarification, or is about to perform an action that requires approval.

At a checkpoint, you can:

- Approve the proposed action
- Edit the proposal or provide reviewer feedback
- Reject the action
- Answer a structured clarification form
- Choose from available actions or options
- Stop the run if the direction has changed

This creates a practical balance between speed and judgment: the assistant can do the detailed work, while people retain control over important decisions.

### 5. Knowledge bases and evidence-oriented work

Reusable knowledge can be organized separately from an individual conversation. Workspace and team knowledge sources can be uploaded, ingested, versioned, published, searched, and explored.

When a conversation uses published knowledge, you can scope the request to selected knowledge bundles or knowledge bases. This helps teams work from an agreed source of truth and makes it clearer which material informed an answer or artifact.

### 6. Documents, reports, and dashboards

Lumo Studio supports more than narrative drafting. It can also help turn structured data into reports and dashboards that live with the rest of the workspace.

Supported workflows include:

- Creating a dashboard artifact from a defined dashboard specification and data package
- Rendering charts and filters in the browser
- Refreshing supported data-backed dashboard and report paths
- Scheduling supported refresh operations
- Reviewing generated output before it becomes part of the durable workspace

The dashboard experience is a native workspace artifact rather than a separate, temporary analytics session.

### 7. Threads, collaboration, and review around the work

Shared workspaces let people discuss and act on specific content instead of exchanging disconnected comments. A thread is the persistent home for one discussion and its work context. It contains the conversation and replies, explicit Lumo requests and responses, linked annotations, relevant file changes, and review decisions.

Collaboration features include:

- Anchored annotations and sticky notes
- Tasks with assignees and due dates
- Change proposals
- Replies and mentions
- Discussion threads
- Follow and read state for team conversations
- Change sets that can be reviewed before application
- Version-aware content and change inspection

This supports a workflow in which one person or an assistant prepares work, others review it in context, and an explicitly approved change set can be applied with a record of what happened. Opening a thread or replying to a message does not itself run Lumo or apply a change.

### 8. Private work and shared review

Shared work and private drafts have different visibility rules. A user can work in an authorized private copy, keep unrelated edits and replies private, and submit only a selected set of changes back to a shared workspace. The submission freezes the reviewed files and revisions so later private edits do not silently change what reviewers saw.

Private annotations also require an explicit sharing action. Sharing discloses the selected excerpt and comment, preserves the relevant immutable file version, and does not publish the private reply history or transcript.

### 9. Governed skills, integrations, and access

Lumo Studio is intended for work where access and repeatability matter. Authenticated workspaces enforce membership and access checks around files, conversations, knowledge, agent runs, and collaboration.

Teams can also manage the capabilities available to the assistant, including skills and configured integrations. Skills can move through private drafting, validation, team submission, review, approval, versioning, and workspace pinning where those workflows are enabled. This gives organizations a way to standardize recurring workflows without giving every task unrestricted access to every system.

## Who Lumo Studio is for

Lumo Studio is useful for people who regularly turn source material into decisions, documents, or shared deliverables, including:

- **Researchers and knowledge workers** who need to synthesize many documents and preserve their findings.
- **Analysts and operations teams** who turn structured data into reports, dashboards, and recurring updates.
- **Writers and document owners** who draft, edit, compare, and publish work with a clear version history.
- **Project and review teams** who need to discuss content, assign follow-up work, and review proposed changes in context.
- **Team leads and administrators** who manage shared knowledge, assistant skills, integrations, permissions, and workspace governance.

It is especially valuable when the work is iterative, source-driven, collaborative, or sensitive enough that people need to see and approve what happens next.

## Example use cases

### Research brief from a document set

Create a workspace, upload a folder of source documents, and ask Lumo to identify the main findings and open questions. The assistant can use the selected files as context, produce a draft brief, and leave the result in the workspace for review and editing.

### Meeting material to follow-up package

Attach or import meeting notes, ask for a summary and follow-up plan, then have Lumo Studio create a draft email, task list, or report. A reviewer can refine the output before it is shared.

### Data-backed report or dashboard

Bring the relevant data into a workspace, ask for an analysis or dashboard, inspect the generated artifact, and schedule a supported refresh when the underlying data changes.

### Collaborative document review

Share a workspace with teammates, add anchored comments or change proposals, discuss them in threads, and review the proposed change set before applying it to the shared work.

### Repeatable team workflow

Use a governed skill for a recurring process such as preparing a standard report or transforming a known document type. Team review and versioning help keep the workflow consistent as it evolves.

## What makes Lumo Studio different

Lumo Studio combines four parts that are often separated:

1. **Context** — the files and knowledge needed to do the work.
2. **Reasoning** — an assistant that can inspect that context and use approved capabilities.
3. **Artifacts** — editable, durable outputs that remain in the workspace.
4. **Accountability** — approvals, versions, access controls, collaboration, and review history.

The goal is not to replace human expertise or turn every workflow into a single prompt. The goal is to reduce the mechanical work around research and production while making the important context, decisions, and revisions easier to follow.

## Product scope

The primary product experience today is the authenticated web application. The mobile directory contains an exploratory prototype rather than a complete mobile client, so mobile authentication, live workspace synchronization, and full mobile chat should not be assumed to be available.

Some integrations, skills, dashboard refresh paths, knowledge workflows, and collaboration experiences may be enabled or governed differently by workspace and deployment. Availability depends on the configured environment, rollout state, and access granted to the user.
